# Learning projects

Routed from `ONBOARD.md` Step 3 when learning a technology is part of the owner's stated purpose.

## Treat learning as a requirement

A project may exist to teach a specific technology. That is a normal owner goal, not an exception inferred from the stack, audience size, or the framework's preference. If the purpose is unclear, ask what the owner wants to be able to do or understand when the project is finished.

Research still applies. Compare ways to reach the learning goal against the owner's cost, time, safety, and operating constraints. A local exercise, a managed service, a hosted platform, and a custom build may teach different parts of the system. Recommend the approach that teaches the named subject with the least irrelevant burden, and explain what it does not teach.

Current evidence matters. Verify the available learning and deployment paths, their limits, and their costs at bootstrap time. Do not assume that a local or cloud path is cheapest, available, or representative from memory.

Record the settled learning goal, the chosen approach and its reason at the project's decision location. Name which components serve the learning goal and which serve the product itself so a later session can evaluate changes without relitigating the purpose.

Learning intent does not waive gates. Real data, external effects, recurring spend, security, and owner authority retain their normal treatment. Measure progress against the stated learning outcome and any user-facing outcome the owner also named.
